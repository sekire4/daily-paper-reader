ABSTRACT 

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) improves the performance of Large Lan-guage Models (LLMs) by retrieving external knowledge. Recently, some RAG methods interleave reasoning and retrieval, enabling LLMs to progressively gen-erate sub-queries and retrieve external knowledge. However, these methods may suffer from redundant or ineffective sub-queries and insufficiently diverse query exploration. Moreover, due to the tightly interleaved nature of reasoning and re-trieval, it remains unclear how the evolving reasoning process influences query intent. To characterize the evolution of query intent during reasoning, we intro-duce search momentum, which captures the accumulated retrieval tendency in-duced by historical changes in generated sub-queries along the reasoning trajec-tory. Through momentum-based analysis, we find that the query generation pro-cess tends to continuously evolve along existing retrieval trajectories. Based on this observation, we propose MAPS, a Macro-Adaptive Parallel Reasoning frame-work for regulating search momentum and mitigating query-intent drift. MAPS first retains the retrieved knowledge and a refined reasoning state, thereby re-ducing the influence of historical reasoning trajectories on subsequent sub-query generation. Subsequently, MAPS extends the conventional sequential reasoning process into a parallel reasoning paradigm. At each iteration, MAPS identifies complementary retrieval directions, generates corresponding sub-queries for par-allel retrieval, and consolidates the retrieved evidence to update the knowledge and reasoning states. Experiments on six question-answering benchmarks demon-strate that MAPS consistently outperforms representative RAG methods across different backbone models. All code and resources are available at https: //github.com/NEUIR/MAPS .

1 INTRODUCTION 

Large Language Models (LLMs) (Jaech et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025) have demonstrated remark-able capabilities in knowledge-intensive question answering and complex reasoning. However, they remain susceptible to hallucinations (Jiang et al., 2023; Ji et al., 2023), leading to factually incor-rect or unsupported responses. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) (Lewis et al., 2020; Izacard et al., 2023) addresses this issue by enabling LLMs to access external knowledge during infer-ence, thereby improving the factuality and reliability of their responses. However, for multi-hop queries (Yang et al., 2018), the required evidence is often distributed across multiple documents and interconnected through dependencies, making single-step retrieval insufficient to comprehensively acquire the information needed to answer the query (Fang et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026). To address this limitation, recent RAG approaches have explored leveraging the reasoning capabil-ities of LLMs to adaptively acquire external evidence. These methods (Li et al., 2025a; Jin et al., 2025; Song et al., 2025) interleave reasoning and retrieval, allowing LLMs to identify missing in-

Specifically, an LLM is prompted to generate sub-queries when additional knowledge is necessary, retrieve relevant documents, and incorporate the retrieved evidence into subsequent reasoning. However, as reasoning and retrieval proceed iteratively, these methods can suffer from redundant or ineffective sub-queries, excessively long retrieval chains, or insufficient exploration diversity (Ji et al., 2025; Martinez et al., 2025), ultimately degrading retrieval and downstream rea-soning performance. Due to the tightly interleaved nature of reasoning and retrieval, it remains unclear how the evolving reasoning process influences the search direction, particularly when com-prehensive evidence requires exploration beyond the current retrieval trajectory. To characterize how search intentions evolve throughout the reasoning process, we introduce search momentum , which captures the accumulated retrieval tendency induced by historical changes in gen-erated sub-queries along the reasoning trajectory. At each step, we update the search momentum by aggregating the current sub-query with the accumulated momentum through a stepwise learnable weight. We learn these weights by using the updated momentum to predict the query-intent drift at the current step. By analyzing the resulting search momentum across reasoning steps, we ob-serve that the deviation directions of subsequent sub-queries become increasingly aligned with the accumulated search momentum as retrieval proceeds deeper. This finding suggests that query gener-ation tends to follow the existing retrieval trajectory, rather than correcting or escaping accumulated deviations through subsequent reasoning (Guo et al., 2025). Such momentum-driven behavior can progressively amplify early search deviations, causing the retrieval process to drift away from the original information need. This motivates us to regulate the accumulation of search momentum and preserve alignment between the evolving retrieval process and the original query intent. In this paper, we propose MAPS, a Macro-Adaptive Parallel Reasoning framework for regulating search momentum and mitigating query-intent drift. MAPS introduces a parallel reasoning paradigm that reduces the risk of search momentum being dominated by a single deviated retrieval direction, thereby maintaining better alignment with the original query intent. Specifically, MAPS first disen-tangles reasoning traces from the sub-queries that propagate historical retrieval tendencies, retaining only the refined retrieval knowledge and reasoning state to reduce the influence of historical search deviations on subsequent query generation. It then extends the conventional sequential reasoning process into a parallel reasoning paradigm, enabling the model to calibrate the search direction and construct a more robust search momentum. Specifically, MAPS identifies multiple complementary retrieval directions from the current reasoning state and generates corresponding sub-queries in par-allel to explore these directions. The resulting evidence is consolidated to update the accumulated knowledge and refined reasoning state, which serves as the basis for the next reasoning iteration. Our experiments on multiple datasets demonstrate that MAPS achieves significant improvements over all baseline methods across different backbone models, highlighting its strong generalization ability and robustness. Further analyses show that MAPS effectively regulates the evolution of search momentum, preventing it from being dominated by a single retrieval direction. Our experi-ments further demonstrate that MAPS effectively mitigates query intent drift and maintains stronger relevance between generated sub-queries and supporting evidence throughout iterative reasoning. Moreover, MAPS enables the model to acquire the prerequisite knowledge required for reasoning at an earlier stage, while reducing unnecessary retrieval iterations. 

2 ANALYZING QUERY INTENT DRIFT DURING LLM R EASONING 

Recent RAG methods (Li et al., 2025a) enable LLMs to actively retrieve relevant documents and incorporate them into the reasoning process, allowing them to progressively acquire the information required to answer a given query. However, the sub-queries generated during this iterative process do not necessarily remain aligned with the original information need. This is because subsequent sub-query generation depends on the preceding retrieval trajectory, which may contain sub-queries deviating from the original information need and noisy retrieved knowledge, thereby influencing the intent of subsequent sub-queries and ultimately degrading downstream reasoning performance (Niu et al., 2026). Uncovering Search Momentum in LLM Reasoning. To characterize how retrieval intent evolves during iterative reasoning, we introduce search momentum , which captures the accumulated seman-tic shift in search behavior across reasoning steps. Given an initial query q0, suppose that the LLM has completed the reasoning trajectory up to the (t − 1) -th step: 

Rt−1 = ( r1, q 1, o 1, . . . , r t−1, q t−1, o t−1), (1) where ri denotes the reasoning step, qi is the generated sub-query, and oi denotes the knowledge observation obtained from the retrieved evidence. Conditioned on the initial query q0 and the pre-ceding reasoning trajectory Rt−1, the LLM M iteratively generates the next reasoning step and sub-query qt until reaching the maximum number of reasoning steps T :

rt, q t = M(q0, R t−1). (2) The generated sub-query qt is then used to retrieve a set of documents Dt, from which the LLM derives the corresponding observation ot. The reasoning trajectory is subsequently updated as: 

Rt = Rt−1 ⊕ (rt, q t, o t). (3) We represent the t-th step query qt by its semantic embedding qt and characterize the evolution of retrieval intent through the semantic displacement between these sub-queries {q0, q 1, . . . , q T }:

qt = Emb( qt), (4) where Emb (·) denotes the embedding model, which can be instantiated with the E5 model (Wang et al., 2022). We then define the search momentum mt at the t-th reasoning step as: 

mt = βtmt−1 + (1 − βt)( qt − qt−1), (5) where m0 = 0 and 0 ≤ βt < 1. The coefficient βt controls how strongly previously accumulated search dynamics are retained when incorporating the latest semantic shift. In this formulation, mt

is an exponentially weighted aggregation of historical query movements along the trajectory q0 →· · · → qt. Its magnitude reflects the strength of the accumulated semantic shift, while its direction characterizes the predominant direction in which the retrieval intent has evolved. 

Tracking Query Intent Drift with Search Momentum. After establishing search momentum as an explicit representation of accumulated query movement, we next examine whether it can predict the direction of subsequent query drift during LLM reasoning. Specifically, if mt is consistently aligned with the deviation of the next sub-query from the original query, it suggests that historical search dynamics are propagated to subsequent query generation and that search momentum can serve as an effective signal for tracking intent drift. For each reasoning trajectory, we therefore measure the directional alignment between the search momentum mt and the deviation of the subsequent sub-query qt+1 from the original query q0:

St = cos( mt, qt+1 − q0), (6) where qt+1 − q0 represents the semantic deviation of the next sub-query from the original infor-mation need. A larger St indicates that the accumulated search dynamics captured by mt are more 3Arxiv Preprint. consistent with the direction in which the subsequent query deviates from the original query, sug-gesting stronger predictive correspondence between search momentum and future query drift. To obtain the momentum at each reasoning step, we fit the coefficient βt in Eq. 5 by maximizing the corresponding directional alignment St. Since βt is constrained to [0 , 1) , we solve the resulting bounded optimization problem using the L-BFGS-B quasi-Newton method (Byrd et al., 1995). Ta-ble 1 reports the momentum coefficients fitted on the training set and the resulting directional align-ment scores St evaluated on a held-out set. The complete procedure is provided in Appendix A.1. We observe a consistent increase in both βt and St as reasoning progresses. The increasing βt indi-cates that later reasoning steps place progressively greater weight on previously accumulated search dynamics, suggesting that historical queries exert an increasingly persistent influence on subsequent query generation as the retrieval process becomes deeper. Meanwhile, the increasing St shows that the next query becomes more aligned with the current search momentum, suggesting that the rea-soning process tends to perpetuate the existing search trajectory rather than effectively correcting or escaping it through reflection (Guo et al., 2025). This observation suggests that the reasoning pro-cess does not naturally eliminate the accumulated search momentum through subsequent reasoning steps. Taken together, these observations motivate us to investigate how to regulate the accumulation and propagation of search momentum, with the goal of reducing the persistent influence of historical search intents and maintaining better alignment with the original information need. 

3 MAPS: R EGULATING INTENT DRIFT THROUGH PARALLEL REASONING 

In this section, we propose MAPS, a Macro-Adaptive Parallel Reasoning-based RAG framework for regulating search momentum. MAPS introduces a parallel reasoning paradigm to reduce the influence of accumulated search momentum and maintain better alignment with the original query intent. Specifically, MAPS first disentangles historical reasoning traces and sub-queries that may propagate previously established retrieval tendencies, thereby reducing their influence on subsequent query generation. It then employs parallel reasoning to explore multiple complementary search directions, enabling the retrieval process to calibrate its search direction at an early stage and further mitigate query intent drift. 

Reasoning Disentanglement for Search Momentum Regulation. Given an initial query q0, exist-ing iterative RAG methods (Li et al., 2025a) maintain a reasoning trajectory Rt−1 accumulated up to the (t − 1) -th iteration: 

Rt−1 = ( r1, q 1, o 1, . . . , r t−1, q t−1, o t−1), (7) where ri, qi, and oi denote the reasoning trace, generated sub-query, and knowledge observation obtained from retrieval at the i-th iteration, respectively. As discussed in Section 2, historical sub-queries can exert a stronger influence on subsequent query generation than the reasoning process itself, causing later queries to inherit and progressively reinforce previously established retrieval tendencies. Such trajectory-dependent propagation can consequently lead to query intent drift. To mitigate this effect, MAPS disentangles the accumulated knowledge from the historical retrieval trajectory by not explicitly preserving the reasoning traces ri and sub-queries qi in Rt−1. Instead, it retains the knowledge acquired over the first t − 1 iterations as a knowledge observation set: 

Ot−1 = ( o1, . . . , o t−1). (8) Meanwhile, rather than retaining the complete sequence of historical reasoning traces r1: t−1 =(r1, . . . , r t−1), MAPS maintains a compact refined reasoning state ˜rt−1 that summarizes the es-sential reasoning information accumulated from previous iterations. This state is updated at each iteration while avoiding explicit exposure to the historical sequence of sub-queries. Accordingly, at the t-th iteration, the subsequent reasoning generated by the model MReason is conditioned on the original query q0, the accumulated knowledge observations Ot−1, and the refined reasoning state ˜rt−1, rather than the complete historical trajectory Rt−1:

(rt, q t, o t) = MReason (q0, O t−1, ˜rt−1), (9) where ˜rt−1 provides the reasoning information necessary for the current iteration, while Ot−1 pre-serves the knowledge acquired from previous retrievals. By removing the explicit dependency on 4Arxiv Preprint. historical sub-queries and reasoning traces, MAPS reduces the propagation of previously accumu-lated retrieval tendencies into subsequent query generation. The reasoning process terminates when the accumulated knowledge Ot = ( o1, . . . , o t−1, o t) satis-fies the current information needs, or when the maximum number of iterations is reached. After the 

T -th iteration, the LLM generates the final answer y based on the original query, the accumulated knowledge observations, and the refined reasoning state: 

y = MReason (q0, O T , ˜rT ), (10) where T denotes the termination iteration or the maximum number of allowed reasoning iterations.

Baselines. We compare MAPS with multiple representative baselines. Vanilla LLM prompts the LLM to generate answers without external knowledge, and Vanilla RAG (Lewis et al., 2020) utilizes the original query to retrieve documents and generates answers based on them. For adaptive RAG models, Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2024) prompts the LLM to determine whether retrieval is needed and whether retrieved documents are used during decoding. For reasoning-driven RAG models, IRCoT (Trivedi et al., 2023) interleaves reasoning with retrieval, where retrieved documents are in-corporated into subsequent reasoning. Search-o1 (Li et al., 2025a) further extends this paradigm by introducing agentic query generation and the Reason-in-Documents module. For sub-query opti-mization RAG models, S3 (Jiang et al., 2025) trains a lightweight model to generate more effective sub-queries. ParallelSearch (Zhao et al., 2025) iteratively decomposes the original query into multi-ple complementary sub-queries and retrieves them in parallel to explore diverse knowledge. 

Implementation Details. We use Qwen3-32B (Yang et al., 2025) and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (Qwen Team, 2026) as the backbone models. The retrieval corpus is the English Wikipedia December 2018 dump (Karpukhin et al., 2020), and we employ E5-base-v2 (Wang et al., 2022) as the dense retriever with FAISS (Douze et al., 2026) for efficient similarity search over the pre-built index. Each retrieval step returns the top-5 most relevant passages, and the maximum number of execution iterations is set to 10. Following previous work (Song et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026), we adopt Cover-EM as the primary evaluation metric.

In this subsection, we conduct ablation studies to evaluate the effectiveness of different components in MAPS. In the experiments, we compare MAPS with three ablated models. MAPS w/o Retrieval Direction directly generates multiple sub-queries in parallel based on the accumulated knowledge and refined reasoning state, without first identifying multiple retrieval directions to guide sub-query generation. MAPS w/o Parallel Reasoning replaces the multi-directional parallel reasoning mech-anism with sequential single-query generation and retrieval, which generates only one retrieval di-rection and its corresponding sub-query at each iteration. MAPS w/o Disentanglement does not dis-entangle the retrieval trajectory, but instead preserves the complete reasoning traces and sub-queries generated at each iteration for subsequent reasoning. As shown in Table 3, MAPS consistently outperforms all ablated variants, demonstrating the effec-tiveness of the proposed framework. Specifically, compared with MAPS w/o Retrieval Direction, MAPS achieves over a 4% improvement, indicating that explicitly identifying complementary re-trieval directions helps guide parallel retrieval toward diverse information needs. MAPS also outper-forms MAPS w/o Parallel Reasoning, demonstrating that the parallel reasoning mechanism expands the search space to acquire supporting evidence at early stages, thereby preventing the subsequent retrieval process from being dominated by a single retrieval direction and causing query intent drift. Moreover, comparing MAPS w/o Disentanglement with MAPS, we observe additional gains, sug-gesting that retaining the complete historical retrieval trajectory introduces stronger dependencies on previous retrieval tendencies, propagating accumulated deviations to subsequent query generation. 5.3 EVOLUTION OF SEARCH MOMENTUM DURING ITERATIVE REASONING

6 RELATED WORK 

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) enhances Large Language Models (LLMs) by retrieving external knowledge during inference (Lewis et al., 2020; Izacard et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025b), thereby providing additional factual support and mitigating hallucinations (Ji et al., 2023). Early RAG systems typically follow a retrieve-then-read paradigm, where the model first retrieves rele-vant documents based on the original query and then feeds the retrieved documents as contextual input to prompt the LLM to generate responses (Shi et al., 2024; Izacard et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024). Furthermore, some studies investigate how to enable LLMs to adaptively determine when to retrieve external knowledge during response generation, thereby improving the reliability of gen-erated responses (Asai et al., 2024). However, for complex questions, retrieving solely with the original query is often insufficient to acquire all relevant evidence, as the required information is typically distributed across multiple documents (Fang et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026). Recent reasoning-driven RAG methods leverage the reasoning capabilities of LLMs to enable pro-gressive reasoning and sub-query generation for acquiring external knowledge, while incorporat-ing the retrieved evidence into subsequent reasoning processes (Li et al., 2025a; Jin et al., 2025). However, these methods heavily rely on the quality of generated sub-queries, where irrelevant or erroneous sub-queries may lead to deviations in subsequent retrieval trajectories. Therefore, some studies have begun to investigate how to optimize the sub-query generation process. S3 (Jiang et al., 2025) trains a lightweight model specifically for sub-query generation, enabling the retrieval of more accurate knowledge. ParallelSearch (Zhao et al., 2025) prompts the model to generate multiple sub-queries at each iteration and performs parallel retrieval, thereby acquiring more comprehensive ev-9Arxiv Preprint. idence. However, these methods do not eliminate the influence of historical retrieval trajectories on subsequent sub-query generation. 

7 CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we introduce search momentum, which captures the accumulated retrieval tendency induced by historical changes in generated sub-queries along the reasoning trajectory. Our exper-iments show that the sub-queries generated by the model tend to continuously evolve along the accumulated retrieval trajectory, leading to deviations in query intent. Based on this observation, we propose MAPS, a Macro-Adaptive Parallel Reasoning framework for regulating search momentum and mitigating query-intent drift. By disentangling historical reasoning trajectories and introducing a parallel reasoning mechanism, MAPS effectively alleviates query-intent drift.

A.1 FITTING THE SEARCH MOMENTUM COEFFICIENTS 

In this section, we provide a detailed description of the experimental settings and methodology for fitting the momentum coefficients introduced in Section 2. Specifically, we first describe how the datasets used for momentum fitting are constructe. Then, we present the detailed procedure for estimating the search momentum coefficients. Finally, we report the fitted momentum results of the MAPS model. 

Data Construction for Momentum Fitting. We collect 1,000 questions from each of 2WikiQA, MuSiQue, and HotpotQA to construct the training data as Dtrain for fitting the momentum coeffi-cients βt. For each question, we run Search-o1 with Qwen3-32B as the backbone model and record the complete trajectory of iterative reasoning and retrieval. We discard trajectories with fewer than three retrieval iterations to retain those containing sufficient query transitions for analyzing accu-mulated search momentum. For each retained trajectory, we encode the original question and all generated sub-queries, {q0, . . . , q T }, using the E5 embedding model, obtaining their semantic rep-resentations {q0, . . . , qT }. To evaluate the directional alignment between search momentum and subsequent query deviations, we collect an additional 1,000 questions from each dataset as Dtest 

, with no overlap with Dtrain , and construct the held-out test data using the same filtering, and encoding procedures as train data do. The search momentum coefficients are fitted exclusively on the training data Dtrain and then fixed for evaluation on the test data Dtest . For the Pooled-MHQA setting, we merge the training data from the three datasets into a pooled training set and similarly combine their test data into a pooled test set.

We then retain the first K alignment scores from each trajectory, where K = 4 , since the vast majority of retrieval trajectories terminate within five iterations. Including later steps would substantially reduce the number of available trajectories at those positions and may lead to unreliable coefficient estimation due to the limited amount of training data.
