Abstract 

Multimodal retrieval-augmented generation over visually rich documents requires evidence at a finer granularity than entire pages. Exist-ing visual retrievers typically index or return full pages, whereas training region-level retriev-ers often requires costly query-to-region anno-tations or noisy model-generated labels. We introduce GARRET, a region-retrieval frame-work trained from (page, question, answer) triplets through differentiable generative feed-back. A shared context-preserving retriever pro-duces page and region multi-vector represen-tations. During training, their retrieval scores define a soft routing distribution over candi-date region crops. An auxiliary vision-language model attends to each crop separately, aggre-gates crop-specific contexts using the routing weights, and predicts the question followed by the answer under a next-token prediction objec-tive. Gradients through the routing distribution assign query-conditioned credit to the indexed region representations, without requiring query-to-region annotations. At inference, the auxil-iary model is removed. GARRET also reuses the contextualized region representations, to-gether with page summary tokens, to construct a compact page representation. We introduce VIDORE-V3-R EGION , a corpus-wide bench-mark containing 200,682 fixed candidate re-gions across eight domains. GARRET achieves a macro nDCG@10 of 0.4126, outperforming the strongest visual baseline by 2.94 points. On page retrieval, it remains comparable to ColQwen2.5-v0.2 while indexing 47.8% fewer vectors per page. 

1 Introduction 

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has natu-rally expanded to visually rich documents, where critical information resides in layouts, tables, and figures (Yu et al., 2025; Tanaka et al., 2025). Re-cent visual retrievers, such as ColPali and ColQwen

(Faysse et al., 2025), directly encode rendered pages to bypass error-prone OCR pipelines, achiev-ing strong page-level performance. However, re-trieving entire pages introduces severe redundancy. The actual evidence for a query is often confined to a short paragraph or a small figure. Feeding full pages into a generation model increases com-putational costs, introduces irrelevant context, and exacerbates hallucination risks. Thus, multimodal RAG demands a finer granularity: retrieving the exact supporting region. Developing robust region-level retrievers, how-ever, faces significant hurdles. Dynamic patch-grouping methods like RegionRAG (Li et al., 2026) construct regions on the fly, precluding efficient of-fline indexing. Conversely, block-based retriev-ers like LFRAG (Zhu et al., 2026) support of-fline indexing but require prohibitively expensive query-to-region annotations. Although large vision-language models (VLMs) can generate pseudo-labels, this process is computationally expensive and often yields noisy supervision. Thus, the lack of scalable, high-quality training data remains the primary bottleneck for fine-grained visual retrieval. To overcome this bottleneck, we leverage the abundant (page , question , answer ) triplets already available in existing datasets. While the exact bounding box is unannotated, the answer provides an implicit supervisory signal: a region is relevant if its visual content enables a model to generate the correct answer. Based on this insight, we pro-pose GARRET ( Generative Answer feedback for

2.2 Generative Supervision for Retrieval 

ReVela connects language modeling with dense re-trieval by optimizing textual document representa-tions through a language-modeling objective, while ElicitR combines contrastive retrieval learning with a generative regularizer (Cai et al., 2026a,b). These methods demonstrate that generative objectives can provide supervision beyond explicit relevance la-bels, but they operate on textual passages, whose retrieval units are naturally defined and share the same modality as the generator. GARRET extends this principle to multimodal region retrieval. Visual regions must be spatially defined, preserve their parent-page context, and remain independently indexable. GARRET treats their relevance as latent and uses soft region routing to connect the retriever with an auxiliary VLM. By jointly predicting the paired question and answer, the generation objective supervises both query– region alignment and evidence sufficiency. Its gradients directly update the persistent region em-beddings retained in the retrieval index, without teacher-generated attention labels, descriptions, or query–region annotations.

3.5 Region-Level Benchmark Construction 

Existing visual document retrieval benchmarks mainly evaluate page-level ranking. Although ViDoRe V3 provides human-annotated evidence boxes, these free-form annotations are intended for localization within relevant pages rather than corpus-wide region retrieval (Loison et al., 2026). We retain the complete query set and eight do-mains of ViDoRe V3, and use MinerU (Wang et al., 2024) to extract query-independent layout bound-ing boxes from every page. Layout boxes that over-lap an original evidence box, i.e., with IoU > 0,are selected as candidate annotations. This pro-cedure produces a fixed corpus of 200,682 layout regions, which are indexed and ranked directly dur-ing evaluation. This setup is consistent with our for-mulation of MinerU regions as persistent retrieval units rather than query-dependent crops. For each overlapping layout box, we draw the box on the full page and ask Qwen3-VL-32B (Bai et al., 2025a) to score its relevance to the query from 0 to 5. Regions with scores greater than 4 are initially labeled positive. Two annotators then independently review all candidate labels. A re-gion is considered relevant only when the content inside the box provides direct or indispensable evi-dence for answering the query; regions with only topical similarity, incidental geometric overlap, or evidence located primarily outside the box are re-jected. Disagreements are discussed until consen-sus is reached. The final human-verified binary labels are used to evaluate corpus-wide region rank-ing with nDCG. 

4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental Setup Training Details. To ensure a fair comparison, we train GARRET exclusively on the official Vi-DoRe training set (118,195 triplets) used by the ColPali and ColQwen series, without relying on external or synthetic data. MinerU (Wang et al., 2024) is solely used for layout parsing. Both the re-triever and the auxiliary VLM are initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (Bai et al., 2025b) with their vision encoders frozen. The retriever uses LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) (rank 64, α = 128 ) and produces 128-dimensional embeddings, while the VLM uses LoRA (rank 32, α = 64 ). We train for 5 epochs on 16 NVIDIA A100 (80GB) GPUs with a learning rate of 1 × 10 −4, and a per-GPU batch size of 24. The number of page summary tokens is set to 100. The NTP loss weight is set to 0.1, and the routing temperature to 0.01. 

Benchmarks and Metrics. We evaluate across three main dimensions: region retrieval, page re-trieval, and downstream generation. For region-level retrieval, we evaluate on our proposed VIDORE-V3-R EGION (2,419 queries, 200,682 re-gions) using nDCG@10 as the primary metric. To assess cross-benchmark generalization, we addi-tionally evaluate on MMDocIR (Dong et al., 2025) for in-document region localization. 4.2 Region-Level Retrieval Performance 

We first evaluate GARRET on our newly con-structed V IDORE-V3-R EGION benchmark, with results presented in Table 1. GARRET estab-lishes a new state of the art with a macro-average nDCG@10 of 41.26, substantially outperforming the strongest vision-based baseline by 2.94 points (Table 1). Unlike baselines that either lose context by encoding isolated crops (ColQwen) or use unstable dynamic regions (RegionRAG), GARRET creates robust representations without costly annotations. This corresponds to 82.3% fewer vectors per region than the ColQwen adapta-tion and a substantially shorter indexed representa-tion. 5 Conclusion 

We introduced GARRET, a novel framework for fine-grained region retrieval in visual documents that does not require manual query-to-region train-ing annotations. GARRET’s core innovation is learning region relevance from generative feedback, where the next-token prediction loss from an aux-iliary VLM provides a powerful, annotation-free training signal. This is realized through a retriever-weighted cross-attention mechanism. Furthermore, our context-preserving multi-granularity embed-ding architecture, a visual analogue to late chunk-ing, creates a unified, layout-aware index for both page and region retrieval, achieving state-of-the-art performance with significantly fewer indexed vectors. On our new large-scale benchmark, GARRET establishes a new state of the art with an nDCG@10 of 0.4126. By enabling the training of high-performance, efficient region retrievers without manual labels, GARRET represents a significant step toward building more scalable, accurate, and cost-effective multimodal information systems.

A.1 Training Data and Candidate Preparation Training Records. Each training record contains a question qi, its answer ai, one paired page d+

Be-fore batching, we reject records with an empty question, an invalid answer, no valid layout can-didate, or anything other than exactly one paired page. When an answer contains multiple accept-able strings, we remove empty entries and join the remaining strings with semicolons. For multiple-choice records whose answer is a letter, we replace the letter with the corresponding option text when that mapping is available. Supervision Boundary. The data preprocessing artifacts may contain legacy positive- and negative-box fields used by other training variants. GAR-RET explicitly clears these fields before the docu-ment forward pass and constructs its region pool ex-clusively from the full candidate list. Consequently, neither the region embeddings nor the routing dis-tribution can access query-to-box labels. The only query-conditioned supervision for region selection is the gradient of the question–answer generation loss. 

Candidate Canonicalization. MinerU provides a fixed ordered list of layout boxes for each page. We express every box as (x1, y 1, x 2, y 2) in the nor-malized [0 , 1000] 2 page coordinate system, clamp its coordinates to the page boundary, and remove entries with non-finite coordinates or non-positive width or height. Valid boxes retain their original relative order. The same canonicalization routine is used by the retrieval-side feature cropper and the feedback-side pixel cropper. Each surviving box is assigned a group index; the tuple consisting of its page index and group index identifies the corre-sponding region tokens, pixel crop, routing weight, and retrieval result. Training aborts if these two candidate views differ in count or order.

B ViDoRe-V3-Region Benchmark Construction 

B.1 Motivation and Source Data 

ViDoRe V3 evaluates retrieval at the page level and additionally provides human-drawn evidence boxes on relevant pages. These evidence boxes are free-form localization annotations: their boundaries may cut through a semantic unit, merge several units, or contain substantial surrounding context. Directly treating them as retrievable regions would therefore make the region corpus query-dependent and would not reflect a realistic indexing setting. We instead construct V IDORE-V3-R EGION by separating region proposal from relevance anno-tation . Region proposals are extracted once from every page without access to any query, while the original evidence boxes are used only to identify regions that require relevance assessment. We retain the complete query set and all eight public domains of ViDoRe V3: human resources (HR), English financial documents (Fin-EN), indus-trial documents, pharmaceutical documents, com-puter science (CS), energy, physics, and French financial documents (Fin-FR). Query text, page identifiers, and the original query–page relevance associations are preserved. The construction pro-cedure below is applied independently within each domain. B.2 Query-Independent Layout-Region Corpus Layout Analysis. We run MinerU on every page image and collect the bounding box of each de-tected layout element. Importantly, MinerU re-ceives only the page and never the query or a Vi-DoRe evidence annotation. Thus, for a page p, it produces a fixed set Bp = {bp, 1, . . . , b p,n p } that can be indexed before any query is observed. 

Box Validation and De-Duplication. We first remove boxes with non-positive width or height. To suppress redundant nested detections, the re-maining boxes are sorted by area in descending order; a box is discarded when at least 90% of its area is covered by a larger box that has already been retained. Duplicate boxes are kept only once per page. This filtering depends only on page lay-out and is therefore independent of the benchmark queries. Each surviving box is assigned a stable region identifier. If no valid layout box remains for a page, a full-page box is used as a fallback so that the page remains searchable. After filtering, the union of the per-page sets forms a fixed corpus of 200 ,682 layout regions. This complete corpus, rather than only regions on relevant pages, is indexed and ranked during eval-uation. Neither VLM scoring nor human review changes the region boundaries or the set of region identifiers. 

B.3 Mapping ViDoRe Evidence to Candidate Layout Regions 

For each official query–page relevance pair, we read the original ViDoRe V3 evidence boxes and express them in the same page coordinate system as the MinerU regions. Let Aq,p denote the set of original evidence boxes for query q on page p. A persistent MinerU region b ∈ B p is selected for annotation if it has positive-area overlap with at least one original evidence box: 

Cq,p = {b ∈ B p | ∃ a ∈ A q,p , IoU( a, b ) > 0} ,

(1) where 

IoU( a, b ) = area( a ∩ b)area( a) + area( b) − area( a ∩ b) .

(2) If a layout region overlaps multiple evidence boxes, it is included only once for that query–page pair. We deliberately use the permissive IoU > 0 rule at this stage to favor recall: an imprecise free-form ev-idence boundary should not prevent a semantically correct layout unit from reaching the subsequent se-mantic and human checks. Conversely, geometric overlap alone never determines the final label. 

B.4 VLM-Assisted Relevance Pre-Annotation Visual Input. Each candidate in Cq,p is indepen-dently assessed in a separate VLM inference call. We draw exactly one candidate box in red on the full page for each model input. The model there-fore scores only one region at a time; an input never contains multiple candidate boxes. This design re-quires many inference calls, but reduces ambiguity about which visual region is being judged. The full page is retained to preserve layout context, while the prompt explicitly requires the score to be based only on content inside the single red box. 

Model and Inference. We use Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct. The model receives the single-box page image and the corresponding query. Before pro-ducing the score, it is required to generate a short reasoning statement that explicitly checks whether the evidence is contained inside the highlighted region. This rationale-first procedure is intended to encourage deliberate region–query comparison. The exact prompt is reproduced in Table 17.

A re-gion is considered relevant only when the content inside the box provides direct or indispensable evi-dence for answering the query. C.3 Candidate-Pool Composition 

The retriever-weighted cross-attention branch pro-cesses every valid candidate from each selected page. Its training memory therefore grows with the number of pages in the local pool. Under our hard-ware configuration, the pool can contain at most three pages per example. The paired page is always included; we vary how the remaining one or two slots are allocated between hard-negative pages and randomly sampled negative pages. A hard-negative page is selected by its highest-scoring candidate, as defined in the main paper, whereas a random page is sampled from the nonpaired pages outside the hard-negative set. With one negative page, hard-negative sampling is substantially more useful for region learning than random sampling (40.39 versus 38.51), because it exposes the router to more confusable candidates. Filling both available negative slots exclusively with hard negatives does not improve further, while the balanced allocation achieves the best region result, 41.26. This suggests complementary roles: hard negatives sharpen fine-grained discrimination and random negatives broaden negative coverage. Across all allocations, page nDCG@10 remains within a narrow 0.24-point range, whereas region nDCG@10 spans 2.75 points. Candidate-pool com-position therefore mainly affects the fine-grained generative-feedback signal rather than the sepa-rately supervised page objective.

D.5 Evaluation Scope 

GARRET ranks regions from a fixed candidate parser. A missed layout candidate therefore limits downstream region recall even when the retriever scores every available candidate correctly. The ViDoRe-V3-Region results measure ranking within this fixed candidate collection. The region qrels use Qwen3-VL scores to initial-ize candidate labels and two annotators to verify all labels. We keep the resulting binary judgments sep-arate from the page qrels and from the MMDocIR localization metrics. The auxiliary VLM increases training cost. GARRET removes it after training, so deployed page and region retrieval use only the query en-coder and precomputed document-side vectors. The vector counts reported in the paper describe representation length; end-to-end indexing time, byte-level storage, and query latency require sepa-rate measurements.
