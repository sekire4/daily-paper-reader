Abstract 

Reasoning-intensive retrieval is fundamentally challenged when queries demand deep seman-tic inference and arrive in multimodal form. Existing systems treat each query in isolation, rely on monolithic rerankers, and lack retriev-ers trained for cross-modal reasoning. We introduce MEMORA 1, a Memory-Enhanced 

Multimodal Orchestrated Reranking Agent built around three contributions: (1) Collab-orative Factor-Committee Reranking , which decomposes queries into intent factors, ex-tracts factor-specific evidence into a struc-tured matrix, and synthesizes judgments via a memory-conditioned meta-verdict model; (2) an Episodic Memory Module that tracks factor outcomes across queries and dynami-cally adjusts priorities at inference time; and (3) a Reasoning-Aware Dense Retriever fine-tuned with chain-of-thought augmentation on 20k + reasoning-intensive pairs. Evaluated on MM-BRIGHT across 29 domains, MEMORA achieves nDCG@10 of 41.5 (text-to-text) and 

38.9 (multimodal query-to-text), outperforming the strongest agentic text-only baseline by +8.3 

points and the strongest agentic multimodal baseline by +7.9 points. 

1 Introduction 

Retrieval systems are increasingly expected to han-dle queries that demand genuine reasoning rather than surface-level matching. This challenge is com-pounded in real-world settings, where users com-bine natural language with images — attaching circuit diagrams to technical questions, screenshots of error logs to debugging requests, or scientific figures to research queries. In these settings, identi-fying the relevant document requires understanding what the image depicts, how it relates to the tex-tual question, and which documents in the corpus  

> 1Code and data are available: https://github.com/ HarnessLab/MEMORA .

collectively address both dimensions. Yet most re-trieval systems are designed for text-only queries, and those that support multimodal input lack the reasoning capacity to handle this level of cross-modal inference. This gap is starkly exposed by MM-BRIGHT (Abdallah et al., 2026c), the first benchmark for reasoning-intensive multimodal retrieval. Despite significant advances in vision-language models, the best multimodal encoder achieves only 27.6 nDCG@10 on the multimodal-to-text task — lower than the best text-only retriever (32.2), meaning that adding visual information actively hurts performance. This counterintuitive result reveals three compounding failure modes that existing approaches have not addressed jointly: (a) Monolithic reranking :existing rerankers apply a single, uniform reason-ing pass over all candidates, regardless of how heterogeneous the query’s underlying information needs are; (b) Stateless retrieval : systems treat every query independently, discarding accumulated knowledge about which retrieval strategies succeed or fail within a domain; and (c) Weak multimodal grounding : standard dense encoders are not trained to handle the abstract, cross-modal reason-ing that multimodal queries demand, producing embeddings where visual signals add noise rather than signal. Prior work addresses at most one of these failures in isolation (Sun et al., 2023; Weller et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2024). We introduce MEMORA (Memory-Enhanced 

Multimodal Orchestrated Reranking Agent), a uni-fied agentic framework that addresses all three failure modes jointly. As illustrated in Figure 1, MEMORA operates through four tightly integrated stages: (1) a Reasoning-Aware Dense Retriever 

encodes queries enriched with chain-of-thought traces to produce a high-quality initial candidate pool; (2) an Episodic Memory Module retrieves arXiv:2610.25282v1 [cs.IR] 18 Oct 2026 similar past queries from domain memory and uses accumulated factor outcome experience to decom-pose the current query into prioritized intent fac-tors; (3) a Collaborative Factor Committee is-sues factor-specific subqueries, extracts targeted evidence per candidate, and organizes judgments into a bipartite factor-document evidence ma-trix that a memory-conditioned meta-verdict model consults to produce the final ranking; and (4) an 

Adaptive Controller dynamically decides whether to expand retrieval, invoke the judge, or stop based on ranking margin and evidence support, making MEMORA responsive to query difficulty rather than fixed in its computation. Our contributions are as follows: • We identify three compounding failure modes in reasoning-intensive multimodal retrieval — monolithic reranking, stateless retrieval, and weak multimodal grounding — and propose 

MEMORA , a unified agentic framework that addresses all three jointly (§3). • We introduce Collaborative Factor-Committee Reranking , in which query intent is decomposed into factors, evidence is extracted per factor and organized into a bipartite factor-document graph, and a memory-conditioned meta-verdict model produces the final ranking (§3). • We propose an Episodic Memory Module 

that accumulates domain-specific factor out-come experience across queries, dynamically adjusting factor priorities at inference time without retraining, and train a Reasoning-Aware Dense Retriever on 20k + reasoning-intensive Stack Exchange pairs with CoT an-notation and hard negatives (§3.2). • The full MEMORA system achieves 41.5 and 

38.9 nDCG@10 on MM-BRIGHT Tasks 1 and 2, outperforming the strongest agentic text-only baseline by +8.3 points and the strongest agentic multimodal baseline by +7.9 

points (§5.1). 

2 Related Work 

Retrieval benchmarks and models. Early benchmarks such as BEIR (Thakur et al., 2021) and MTEB (Muennighoff et al., 2023) primarily measure surface-level relevance. BRIGHT (Su et al., 2025) and RAR-b (Xiao et al., 2024) made reasoning a core requirement and exposed sharp performance drops in existing systems. MM-BRIGHT (Abdallah et al., 2026c) extends this setting to multimodal queries, and the concur-rent benchmarks MRMR (Zhang et al., 2025) and ARK (Lin et al., 2026) confirm that visual and spatial reasoning remain persistent bottlenecks. Dense retrievers such as E5 (Wang et al., 2024), GritLM (Muennighoff et al., 2024), and SFR-Embedding (Meng et al., 2024) perform well on standard benchmarks but degrade when relevance depends on reasoning. ReasonIR (Shao et al., 2025) and DiVeR (Sun et al., 2025) partially close this gap through reasoning-aware training. Multimodal retrievers, including VLM2Vec (Jiang et al., 2024), GME (Zhang et al., 2024), Argus (Abdallah et al., 2026a), and Nomic-Vision (Nussbaum et al., 2024), extend dense retrieval to vision-language inputs. However, their best result on MM-BRIGHT Task 2 (27.6 nDCG@10) still falls below the strongest text-only baseline, indicating that visual signals introduce noise unless they are accompanied by explicit reasoning. 

Multimodal reasoning-intensive retrieval pipelines. A recent line of work addresses this gap by inserting explicit reasoning be-tween the multimodal query and the retriever. BRIDGE (Mounis et al., 2026) reformulates multimodal queries into text queries aligned with text retrievers, learning this alignment through reinforcement learning. HIVE (Abdalla et al., 2026a) adopts a query–hypothesize–verify loop in which an LLM proposes candidate hypotheses and checks them against retrieved evidence. MARVEL (Kasem et al., 2026) combines adaptive query expansion with reranking in a unified expand–rerank–retrieve pipeline, while VISA-Agent (Abdalla et al., 2026b) converts visual content into symbolic representations that an agent can reason over during retrieval. Together, these methods show that translating visual content into reasoning-friendly forms is more effective than embedding it directly. 

Reranking, query expansion, and memory. 

LLM-based rerankers (Abdallah et al., 2025; Zhuang et al., 2023; Abdallah et al., 2026b; Pradeep et al., 2023) substantially outperform dense retrieval alone. Query expansion meth-ods (Gao et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023; Lei et al., 2025) and agentic systems (Trivedi et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2026) improve recall through LLM-generated subqueries and interleaved reasoning. Memory-augmented approaches draw on episodic experience for LLMs (Fountas et al., 2024), case-based RAG (Wiratunga et al., 2024), and multi-modal episode indexing (Xie et al., 2024). Despite this progress, no prior work addresses all three failure modes jointly: existing rerankers are mono-lithic, retrievers are stateless, and no system uni-fies episodic factor memory with committee-based reranking under multimodal conditions. 

3 Method 

MEMORA is a memory-conditioned retrieval agent that treats each query q = ( t, x ) — text t and optional image x — as a composition of latent in-tent factors. Given a corpus D = {dj }Nj=1 , the goal is to produce a ranking π(q) = ( d(1) , . . . , d (K))

that maximizes relevance to the full user intent. Unlike fixed prompt pipelines, MEMORA per-sistently stores which factors helped or failed in previous queries and uses this signal to guide both retrieval and ranking. 

3.1 Overview 

The pipeline proceeds in four stages: (1) a reasoning-aware dense retriever produces an ini-tial candidate pool; (2) domain memory episodes guide decomposition of the query into intent fac-tors; (3) factor-specific subqueries expand the pool and a factor-document evidence graph is con-structed; (4) a factor-aware scorer ranks candidates, with a memory-aware listwise judge invoked when needed.

4 Experiments 

4.1 Dataset 

We evaluate on MM-BRIGHT (Abdallah et al., 2026c), the first reasoning-intensive multimodal retrieval benchmark, comprising 2,803 queries 

across 29 technical domains in four categories (STEM & Life Sciences, Software & Technical Sys-tems, Social Sciences & Humanities, and Applied Domains) over a corpus of 2.5M documents from Stack Exchange. We evaluate on Task 1 (text-to-text) and Task 2 (multimodal query-to-text, where queries include diagrams, charts, and screenshots), using nDCG@10 as the primary metric. To prevent training-test contamination, we con-structed MEMORA-R’s training set by excluding all Stack Exchange threads whose post IDs appear in the MM-BRIGHT document corpus, as well as any threads posted after the benchmark’s data collection cutoff. The resulting training split is temporally and ID-disjoint from both the retrieval corpus and the query set used for evaluation.

6 Conclusion 

We presented MEMORA, a memory-enhanced multimodal reranking agent addressing three com-pounding failures in reasoning-intensive retrieval: monolithic reranking, stateless retrieval, and weak multimodal grounding. MEMORA achieves 41.5 

and 38.9 nDCG@10 on MM-BRIGHT Tasks 1 and 2, outperforming the strongest agentic text-only baseline by +8.3 points and the strongest agentic multimodal baseline by +7.9 points across all 29 domains. Future work will explore ro-bust factor generation under lexically ambiguous queries and cross-domain memory transfer for low-resource settings. 

Limitations 

MEMORA relies on GPT-4o for factor decompo-sition, evidence extraction, and the meta-verdict judge, which introduces API cost and latency that may limit deployment in low-resource settings. The episodic memory requires a warm-up period of 

>50 queries per domain before yielding its largest gains, making it less effective for rare or newly-added domains. The Reasoning-Aware Dense Re-triever is trained on Stack Exchange data and may not generalize equally to domains with substan-tially different writing styles. Finally, the adap-tive controller uses a fixed linear decision rule that, while interpretable, may be suboptimal compared to a learned policy.

C Analysis 

Effect of candidate pool size. Figure 4 shows T1 nDCG@10 as a function of K0. Performance increases from 38.0 ( K0 = 25 ) to 41.5 ( K0 =50 ) and 43.0 ( K0 = 75 ), with diminishing returns beyond the default K0 = 50 . The +3.5 gain from 25 to 50 reflects a higher-quality candidate pool for the factor committee, while the smaller +1.5 gain from 50 to 75 suggests marginal documents at larger pool sizes add noise to evidence extraction. We adopt K0 = 50 as the default. 

Failure modes. Two recurring patterns emerge from per-domain results. First, high-ambiguity domains — Crypto (T1: 18.9 vs. base 24.3) and GIS (T1: 28.4 vs. 31.7) — see MEMORA un-derperform the base retriever, as the factor plan-ner generates semantically overlapping subqueries that introduce noise rather than diversity. Sec-ond, low-resource domains with fewer than 10 stored episodes (e.g., Quantum Computing, Phi-losophy) show modest memory gains, confirming that episodic memory requires sufficient query vol-ume to learn reliable factor priors. Both failure modes point to natural future directions: more ro-bust factor generation under ambiguous queries, and cross-domain memory transfer to bootstrap low-resource domains.
