## Abstract 

Multilingual multi-hop document question answering (MMHop-DocQA) requires locating and integrating evidence across com-plex layouts, document pages, and diverse languages. Existing ap-proaches include single-pass processing and adaptive tool use, but learning to coordinate evidence acquisition, cross-page integration, and answer verification remains challenging. To address this chal-lenge, we present DocSkill , an agentic framework with a learned skill-orchestration policy for adaptive evidence acquisition and rea-soning . The policy selects, revisits, or skips skills according to the evolving evidence state. To construct supervision for skill orchestra-tion, bounded Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) explores alternative skill trajectories offline. These trajectories provide demonstrations for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and preference pairs for Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). We further introduce MMHop-DocBench , a multilingual multi-hop document QA dataset with training and test splits, containing 60K QA pairs across 10 lan-guages and eight document domains. Its HopGen pipeline builds hierarchical page–element graphs to derive traceable cross-page evidence paths and generate grounded QA pairs with page- and element-level evidence annotations. Experiments show that Doc-Skill with Qwen3-VL-8B achieves 41.0 accuracy and 49.2 F 1 on MMHop-DocBench, exceeding the strongest evaluated baseline by 5.5 and 6.3 points, respectively, and achieving competitive perfor-mance on MMLongBench-Doc and DocBench.

## 1 Introduction 

Multilingual multi-hop document question answering (MMHop-DocQA) is a cornerstone capability for intelligent systems that process multilingual, multi-page documents [16, 22, 24, 35, 37, 49]. The fundamental challenge lies in locating and integrating sparse, spatially distributed evidence across heterogeneous layouts and lan-guages, requiring iterative document navigation, structural ground-ing, and multi-step reasoning over long contexts. 

Figure 1: Overview of DocSkill and its comparison with VLM-based and tool-augmented methods. DocSkill adaptively or-chestrates four skills, Locate , Inspect , Integrate , and Verify ,for cross-page document reasoning and answering. 

As illustrated in Figure 1, recent approaches can be broadly grouped into two paradigms with progressively increasing reason-ing capabilities, yet each exhibiting inherent limitations. (a) VLM-based Methods directly encode document pages and queries into a Vision-Language Model (VLM) [3, 4, 11, 27]. Although these meth-ods benefit from end-to-end multimodal understanding, single-pass inference over long document contexts can suffer from context over-load, language inconsistency, and incomplete integration of sparse evidence across pages. (b) Tool-Augmented Methods enhance document processing through OCR [9], retrieval-augmented gener-ation [6, 10, 12, 45], or agentic tool use [13, 17, 33, 46]. While these mechanisms improve evidence access and support adaptive tool use, learning to jointly coordinate evidence acquisition, cross-page integration, and answer verification remains challenging. These arXiv:2610.36759v1 [cs.CL] 27 Oct 2026 Jing Huang et al. 

limitations are further amplified in multilingual settings, which re-quire robust understanding across languages and reliable evidence grounding throughout the reasoning process. To address these limitations, we introduce DocSkill , an agentic framework for learned skill orchestration in multilingual multi-hop document QA. Its key design shifts agent control from operation-level tool selection to capability-level skill orchestration. Specifi-cally, we distinguish a skill from a tool : a skill is a reusable capability abstraction that specifies what the agent should accomplish given the current interaction state, whereas a tool is a concrete executable operation that specifies how part of that capability is carried out. Accordingly, a skill may combine model-native reasoning with one or more tool calls, decoupling orchestration decisions from specific tool implementations. As illustrated in Figure 1, DocSkill organizes document interaction into four reusable skills: Locate combines query refinement with page retrieval via Page_Retriever; Inspect 

extracts and interprets fine-grained multimodal content, invoking Page_Parser or Zoom_In when needed; Integrate connects comple-mentary findings across pages; and Verify checks the grounding and consistency of candidate answers against accumulated evidence. Conditioned on the evolving interaction state, the learned policy adaptively selects, skips, repeats, and composes these skills. Learning adaptive skill orchestration requires trajectory-level supervision because the effectiveness of each skill decision depends on its downstream contribution to evidence acquisition, reasoning, and answer generation. We adopt an offline trajectory curation and preference optimization strategy. We use bounded Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) [31, 43] to explore alternative trajectories over the skill space. The resulting trajectories are ranked by answer cor-rectness and overall quality, assessed through language consistency, logical soundness, and skill-orchestration effectiveness, to construct training data for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and trajectory-level preference pairs. We optimize the orchestration policy through SFT followed by Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) [5, 29, 40], which learns from preferred and dispreferred skill trajectories. To address the scarcity of multilingual multi-hop document QA data, we introduce MMHop-DocBench with train and test splits to support policy learning and benchmark evaluation. The dataset is constructed using HopGen , an automatic curation pipeline that rep-resents long documents as hierarchical Page–Element Graphs [14, 39] and derives traceable cross-page evidence paths for grounded question generation. MMHop-DocBench contains 60K question– answer pairs across 10 languages and 8 document domains, together with explicit page- and element-level evidence annotations. Experiments show that DocSkill outperforms strong baselines on MMHop-DocBench and achieves competitive performance on both MMLongBench-Doc and DocBench. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: 

• Agentic Skill-Orchestration Framework. We introduce DocSkill, which orchestrates four reusable reasoning and tool-use skills. Bounded MCTS generates trajectories for SFT and preference pairs for DPO. 

• Multilingual Multi-Hop QA Dataset. We introduce Hop-Gen to construct MMHop-DocBench, comprising 60K QA pairs across 10 languages and eight domains with page- and element-level evidence annotations. 

• Empirical Results and Analysis. Experiments on three benchmarks evaluate DocSkill against strong baselines, with comparisons and ablations analyzing skill orchestra-tion and trajectory supervision. 

## 2 Related Work 2.1 Document Question Answering 

Document Question Answering (DocQA) involves extracting and reasoning over textual and visual evidence in documents. VLM-based approaches process document images directly [4, 11, 27], while OCR-based pipelines [9] extract text and layout informa-tion for subsequent reasoning. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) [6, 10, 12, 45] selects relevant evidence from searchable doc-ument indexes to support answer generation. Document agents extend these approaches through iterative evidence acquisition and tool use [13, 17, 33, 48]. DeepRead [21] supports structure-aware retrieval and section reading, while ORCA [19] coordinates spe-cialized agents through routing and debate. Doc-𝑉 ∗ [47] combines OCR-free page navigation with working memory, using SFT and GRPO for policy training. Our work focuses on multilingual multi-hop DocQA, learning state-dependent orchestration over capability-level skills through SFT and DPO on offline MCTS trajectories. 

## 2.2 Skill-integrated Agentic Reasoning 

Skill learning for LLM agents focuses on acquiring structured rea-soning and action strategies. Prior work, including ReAct [44] and tool-oriented approaches such as Toolformer [30], ToolAlpaca [36], and Gorilla [28], demonstrates the effectiveness of interleaving rea-soning with tool use. These approaches establish reasoning and tool-use interfaces. Some recent works have explored skill-integrated agent frameworks, such as AutoSkill [42] and XSkill [18], where task-level skills and action strategies are explicitly defined and com-posed to solve complex tasks. In this work, we introduce DocSkill, a skill-integrated agentic framework for multilingual multi-hop document question answering that dynamically composes reason-ing actions and tool usage based on evolving interaction states for iterative evidence acquisition and structured document reasoning.

Verify uses model reasoning to assess the candidate answer for completeness, consistency, and evidence support. Its feedback guides further evidence acquisition and answer revision. 

3.1.4 Execution and Composition. Executing a skill returns an ob-servation, updates the relevant state components, and records the action and observation in the interaction history. Trajectories retain the agent’s recorded reasoning, skill invocations, and native skill outputs, including candidate answers and verification feedback. Na-tive skill outputs serve as observations that condition subsequent decisions. Tools are invoked within skills rather than exposed as independent top-level actions. Skills compose sequentially through the shared state. At each step, the policy selects a skill invocation or a terminal answer. The updated state allows skills to be revisited or skipped without a prescribed order. These sequences are selected dynamically rather than defined as separate compound skills. ## 3.2 Offline MCTS-Based Skill Trajectory Curation 

The parameterized interactions defined above induce a large tra-jectory space, where the value of a skill invocation may depend on its downstream effects on evidence acquisition, integration, and verification. Independent rollouts can repeatedly explore similar paths and provide limited coverage of alternative decisions. We therefore apply bounded Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) [31, 43] to allocate a fixed search budget across candidate skill trajectories. For each training instance 𝑥 𝑖 = (D 𝑖 , 𝑞 𝑖 ), the root node 𝑛 0 rep-resents the initial state 𝑧 0, and each subsequent node represents an interaction state. An edge records a complete action. For a skill invocation 𝑎 𝑡 = (𝑠 𝑡 , 𝜉 𝑡 ), executing the edge produces an observation 

𝑜 𝑡 +1 and transitions the agent from 𝑧 𝑡 to 𝑧 𝑡 +1. A terminal answer action ends the corresponding branch. Search depth counts policy decisions, with each edge representing one skill invocation or ter-minal answer. Each edge 𝑒 maintains an estimated value 𝑄 (𝑒 ) and a visit count 𝑁 (𝑒 ), while each node 𝑛 maintains a visit count 𝑁 (𝑛 ).

## 4 MMHop-DocBench 

We introduce MMHop-DocBench, a large-scale benchmark for mul-tilingual multi-hop document question answering. It contains long documents from diverse domains and provides page- and element-level evidence references for each question–answer pair. The bench-mark is constructed using HopGen , an automatic pipeline that gener-ates grounded questions from traceable cross-page evidence paths. 

## 4.1 HopGen: MMHop-DocQA Pair Curation 

Figure 3 provides an overview of HopGen . HopGen uses GPT-5.6-SOL [27] for both generation and evaluation, with role-specific prompts and separate inference contexts, while PP-OCR [9] pro-vides layout-aware parsing to extract fine-grained elements from document pages. For each document, the generator creates a compact profile of every page, including its summary, entities, topics, and layout struc-tures. It then assesses candidate page pairs for explicit semantic 

Figure 3: Overview of HopGen, which constructs hierarchical page–element graphs to generate and verify multilingual multi-hop QA pairs with explicit evidence references. 

dependencies to construct a Page Graph. The parser extracts fine-grained document elements and attaches them to their correspond-ing source pages, forming a hierarchical Page–Element Graph. Con-nected page sequences and their associated elements define candi-date cross-page evidence paths for grounded question generation and subsequent verification. Given an evidence path and parsed elements, the generator produces questions, answers, and support-ing page–element references in the document language using only the supplied evidence. The generator is instructed to create ques-tions requiring complementary evidence from at least two distinct pages, with no single page sufficient for the complete answer. The evaluator scores candidates for answer correctness, evidence suffi-ciency, cross-page necessity, and language consistency, retaining those meeting a predefined threshold. Normalized question–answer duplicates are resolved by retaining the higher-scoring candidate. 

## 4.2 Dataset Statistics 

MMHop-DocBench contains 60K question–answer pairs spanning ten languages: English (EN), Chinese (ZH), Cantonese (YUE), In-donesian (ID), Burmese (MY), Filipino (TL), Tamil (TA), Thai (TH), Vietnamese (VI), and Malay (MS). The documents cover eight do-mains: Business and Finance, Government, Academic, Education, Technical, Medical, Legal, and Media. Within the comparison in Table 1, MMHop-DocBench provides the widest language coverage and the highest average page count, at 68.2 pages per document. Each pair includes page- and element-level evidence annotations, enabling evaluation of evidence localization and answer grounding. 

## 4.3 Human Audit of Dataset Quality 

We conduct a human quality audit of 1,200 retained QA pairs, sam-pling 80 training and 40 test pairs from each of the ten languages, with stratification across document domains. Two target-language annotators independently assess each pair for question validity, reference-answer correctness, evidence sufficiency, and cross-page necessity. Disagreements are adjudicated by a third annotator. Of the 1,200 audited pairs, 1,144 (95.3%) satisfy all four criteria, while 56 (4.7%) fail at least one.

## 5.1 Main Results 

Results on MMHop-DocBench. Table 2 reports results on MMHop-DocBench. In same-backbone comparisons across Gemini-3.1-Pro [11], Claude-Sonnet-5 [3], and GPT-5.6-SOL [27], DocSkill improves over the corresponding DocLens [48] variants by 2.3–2.5 Acc. and 2.4– 2.7 F 1 points. DocSkill with Qwen3-VL-8B [4], trained through SFT followed by DPO [29], achieves the highest macro-averaged scores among the evaluated model configurations: 41.0 Acc. and 49.2 F 1. It exceeds DocLens with GPT-5.6-SOL by 5.5 Acc. and 6.3 F 1 points. This comparison evaluates complete configurations with different backbones and training conditions. Human experts achieve 61.7 Acc. and 63.9 F 1 on the sampled test subset, providing a reference. 

Generalization to Public Benchmarks. Table 3 reports results on MMLongBench-Doc and DocBench. DocSkill with Qwen3-VL-8B, trained on MMHop-DocBench through SFT followed by DPO, achieves 69.2 Acc., 67.3 F 1, and 68.9 LASJ on MMLongBench-Doc, and 86.8 LASJ on DocBench. Compared with the strongest competing base-line listed for each metric, these results yield gains of 1.4, 10.5, 0.7, and 1.3 points, respectively. Using GPT-5.2 as the controller, Doc-Skill exceeds the reported results of DocDancer [46] by 11.5 Acc., 10.0 F 1, and 0.3 LASJ on MMLongBench-Doc, and by 0.4 LASJ on DocBench. 

## 5.2 Qualitative Analysis 

Figure 4 illustrates a case from a 67-page Indonesian document. The question requires combining complementary evidence from text and tables across pages 18, 31, and 38. DocAgent searches and inspects pages sequentially but stops after collecting incomplete evidence from pages 17 and 31. Consequently, it introduces unsup-ported content and cites incorrect evidence. In contrast, DocSkill gathers and integrates evidence across all required pages and veri-fies the answer, enabling complete, grounded cross-page reasoning.

## 6 Conclusion 

We presented DocSkill, a document agent that learns to orches-trate four reusable capability-level skills for multilingual multi-hop reasoning. Bounded MCTS constructs skill trajectories for SFT and DPO. We also introduced MMHop-DocBench, containing 60K grounded QA pairs across 10 languages and eight document do-mains, with page- and element-level evidence annotations. Experi-ments on MMHop-DocBench, MMLongBench-Doc, and DocBench demonstrate consistent improvements over strong baselines, while ablations support skill abstraction, adaptive orchestration, and tra-jectory supervision. Future work will expand language and do-main coverage and explore automatic discovery and refinement of reusable document skills for more complex document scenarios.

## B More Details of HopGen B.1 HopGen Construction Pipeline 

HopGen automatically constructs grounded multilingual multi-hop QA pairs from long documents. Algorithm 1 formalizes the auto-matic curation procedure, comprising hierarchical graph construc-tion, cross-page evidence-path extraction, candidate generation, and quality-based filtering. The Page Graph captures semantic dependencies between page profiles, while the Page–Element Graph grounds them in localized text, tables, figures, captions, and equations. Each evidence path spans two to four pages, with edges retained only when supported by explicit semantic and grounded element-level relations. HopGen generates at most three QA candidates per path. The evaluator scores answer correctness, evidence sufficiency, cross-page neces-sity, and language consistency, retaining candidates with threshold 

𝜏 = 0.875. After question and answer normalization, duplicates are merged by retaining the highest-scoring candidate. Each final QA pair preserves its supporting page and element annotations.
