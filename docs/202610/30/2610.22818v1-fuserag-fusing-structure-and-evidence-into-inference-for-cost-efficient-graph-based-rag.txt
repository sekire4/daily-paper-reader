# FuseRAG: Fusing Structure and Evidence into Inference for Cost-Efficient Graph-based RAG 

Tingqi Wang, Yingshu Li, Zhipeng Cai *

Department of Computer Science, Georgia State University 

Atlanta, GA, United States 

{twang33, yili, zcai }@gsu.edu 

Abstract —Graph-based retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) exploits relationships among evidence to support multi-hop ques-tion answering. However, conveying retrieved graphs through non-unique linear textual contexts to Large Language Models (LLMs) leads to graph-structure degradation and inefficient KV-cache reuse. Meanwhile, graph-structure order optimization is already NP-hard, and the additional KV-cache reuse objective further complicates the search by introducing potentially con-flicting orders. To tackle these issues, we propose FuseRAG, a cost-efficient graph-based RAG framework that shifts most retrieved evidence from explicit textual contexts to latent rep-resentations. Our main idea is to separate reusable evidence content from query-aware structural interactions in latent space, while retaining only a few explicit facts as semantic anchors. The resulting latent representations are injected into decoder self-attention, where they reshape attention over evidence and thereby influence hidden states and next-token probabilities. This design preserves graph structure through latent interactions, enables evidence reuse beyond exact token-prefix matching. Experiments on three multi-hop question-answering datasets with Llama-3-8B and Qwen3.5-9B show that FuseRAG improves answer quality while achieving up to 2.7 × and 2.2 × TTFT speedups over the fastest graph-based baseline, respectively. 

Index Terms —Graph-based RAG, Large Language Model 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Large Language Models (LLMs) [1], [2] have demonstrated significant capabilities in language understanding, greatly ad-vancing the development of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Despite their considerable success, LLMs still generate hallucinations and wrong answers due to the lack of domain-specific knowledge in pretraining data [3]–[6]. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) [7] methods have been widely adopted to mitigate this limitation by integrating external knowledge into LLMs [8]. Among these approaches, graph-based RAG [9]–[13] has attracted substantial attention. It constructs a knowledge graph from source documents and retrieves relevant entities and relations as evidence to aug-ment the LLM context. Graph captures complex relationships among entities, facilitating multi-hop reasoning along rela-tional paths. Although benefiting from graph-structure knowledge, exist-ing graph-based RAG still suffers from limited expressiveness. Specifically, before being fed into LLMs, the retrieved graph-structured evidence must be serialized into linear textual con-texts. This mechanism poses a major challenge to graph-based RAG: Graph Structure Degradation. After serialization, the

In contrast, factual entities separated by irrelevant content break the subgraph relationships and require LLMs to reason answers from multiple confusing information across distant contextual positions. Consequently, reasoning paths become harder to trace, even with the same retrieved results. Furthermore, the variability serialization of evidence hin-ders the KV-cache reuse efficiency . RAG typically introduces a large amount of external knowledge, increasing the prefill arXiv:2610.22818v1 [cs.DB] 16 Oct 2026 cost. As a common solution, KV-cache reuse accelerates LLM inference by caching the key-value states of previously processed tokens and reusing them. However, these approaches are inherently designed for sequential text and assume identi-cal token prefixes [15], [16]. In graph-based RAG, inconsistent serialization orders undermine KV-cache reuse across equiva-lent evidence contexts and increasing inference cost. The above issues motivate a method that effectively exploits graph structure while maintaining inference efficiency. An intuitive idea is to optimize the serialization order for both graph-structure representation and KV-cache reuse. Neverthe-less, as shown in Section II, structural ordering is already NP-hard, and the additional cache-reuse objective further compli-cates the optimization. The search cost grows rapidly with the graph size, making optimal serialization impractical for large-scale graph-based RAG. Therefore, we explore an alternative to textual serialization for conveying graph evidence to LLMs, aiming to alleviate these limitations. In LLMs, queries and retrieved evidence ultimately interact through attention over their internal representations. Inspired by this mechanism, we embed query-evidence interactions into latent space and integrate it with the LLM’s internal represen-tations, avoiding information missing in textual serialization. To this end, we propose FuseRAG ( FU sing Structure and 

Evidence into transformer inference), a novel framework for efficient graph-based RAG, which shift the primary evidence interface from textual serialization to latent representations. FuseRAG consists of three stages: In the stage 1, FuseRAG retrieves query-aware evidence from the knowledge graph with greedy heuristic search, preserving the retrieved sub-graph structure for subsequent fusion. In the next stage, dual-Path Evidence Fusion embeds the retrieved evidence into latent space and constructs compact explicit evidence. Cross-attention between the query and evidence tokens produces query-specific node representations , which are further fused by graph propagation, obtaining an auxiliary state for each node. These auxiliary states are appended to the original evidence states as structural supplements. In parallel, from the retrieved subgraph, a lightweight scoring mechanism selects a small set of facts based on their relevance to the query. To maximize the total relevance score under a strict token budget of 32, 64, or 128 tokens, the 0-1 Knapsack Algorithm is adopted for selection. In Stage 3, FuseRAG projects the node representations into memory KV, which are integrated with text KV in the decoder’s self-attention. Node representations remain reusable across queries, while memory KV is reused throughout each answer generation. By integrating reusable evidence representations and query-aware graph interactions into LLM attention, FuseRAG moves beyond purely textual context construction. This design enables faster inference and more accurate answer generation in graph-based RAG. Our contributions are summarized as follows:

II. P RELIMINARIES AND MOTIVATION 

A. Graph-based RAG 

Let G = ( V, E) denote a knowledge graph constructed from text corpus T , where V and E are the sets of nodes and edges. Each node v ∈ V has a corresponding textual evidence unit 

xv . Given a query q, a graph-based RAG system retrieves a subgraph Gq = ( Vq , Eq ) from G, where Vq ⊆ V and Eq ⊆ E .

Gq with supplementary information enhances LLM generation. Since LLMs take sequential textual inputs, conventional graph-based RAG methods serialize Gq according to an order π =(π1, π 2, . . . , π |V q |). Therefore, the textual context is written as 

Xπ = Serialize( Gq , π ). Then, LLM generates an answer y

with system instructions, query q and evidence context Xπ .Naturally, a retrieved subgraph Gq has multiple serialization orders. LLMs exhibit positional biases in long contexts, uti-lizing information near the beginning or end more effectively [14]. Moreover, the connected evidence in Gq becomes more difficult to integrate when scattered across distant positions in the serialized context. Consequently, although these orders preserve the same underlying graph evidence, different textual sequences and relevant positions lead to substantial differences in LLM inference. 

B. KV-Cache Reuse in LLM Inference 

Mordern decode-only LLMs employ multiple Transformer layers. Each layer mainly consists of a self-attention module and a feed-forward network (FFN). At l-th layer, hidden representations H(l) are projected into queries Q(l), keys K(l),and values V (l) with trainable weights. Self-attention aggre-gates value representations according to query-key relevance, capturing contextual information among tokens. The resulting representations are further transformed by the FFN before being passed to the next layer. LLM inference stage contains two phases: prefill and decod-ing [17]. During prefill, LLMs process the entire input context in parallel and compute key-value states for all Transformer layers, which are stored in the KV cache for subsequent decoding. The prefill cost increases with the context length, as longer inputs require more attention computation. This overhead is particularly pronounced in RAG, where retrieved evidence constitutes the majority of the input context [18]. In the decoding phase, LLMs generate tokens autoregressively. At each step, LLM reuses the cached key-value states of previous tokens and computes only new generated token. Beyond within request reuse, if the current input shares an identical token prefix with a previously request, the corresponding cached key-value states can be directly reused to avoid repeated prefill computation. This mechanism significantly reduce redundant computation in LLM inference. However, KV-cache reuse requires exact prefix matching. In graph-based RAG, same evidence admits multiple serialization order, resulting in dif-ferent token prefixes and fewer opportunities for cache reuse [19].

III. M ETHODOLOGY 

In this section, we propose FuseRAG, which shifts the primary representation of graph evidence from textual seri-alization to latent representations, preserving graph structure and enabling efficient KV-cache reuse simultaneously. Next, we describe its design in detail. 

A. Overview 

Following the execution flow, FuseRAG consists of three stages: Evidence Retrieval, Dual-Path Evidence Representa-tion, and LLM Integration:  

> •

Stage 1: Evidence Retrieval. Given a query q and a knowledge graph G, FuseRAG retrieves a set of relevant evidence Bq together with the corresponding subgraph 

Gq . FuseRAG is compatible with general graph retrieval methods. In our implementation, we adopt a greedy heuristic search strategy to construct (Bq , Gq ). Specifi-cally, a lightweight scorer sequentially selects evidence according to the query and selected evidence. Fig. 2: Overview of FuseRAG. The framework consists of three stages: evidence retrieval, dual-path evidence representation, and LLM integration. Reusable latent evidence and graph interactions are integrated into decoder self-attention through additional memory key-value states, while compact explicit facts provide semantic anchors.  

> •

Stage 2: Dual-Path Evidence Representation. 

FuseRAG represents the retrieved evidence through a latent path and an explicit path. The latent path independently encodes each retrieved evidence unit into reusable token-level representations. These representations are then pass messages by graph encoder over the retrieved subgraph to capture query-relevant structural dependencies. The resulting latent memory Mq therefore combines detailed evidence semantics with query-aware graph information without requiring the full retrieved graph to be serialized as text. In parallel, the explicit path extracts a compact set of query-aware facts Sq under a strict token budget, providing concise semantic anchors to the LLM. Since only a small amount of evidence is serialized into text, the positional effects associated with long contexts are less pronounced.  

> •

Stage 3: LLM Integration. FuseRAG incorporates the two evidence representations into LLM inference. The latent memory Mq is projected into memory key-value states with an adapter and then combined with the prompt key-value states in self-attention. The compact evidence 

Sq is concatenated with the query to form the prompt. During decoding, the LLM attends to both the latent memory and the prompt context to compute the prob-ability distribution over the next token. In addition, we describe the training and inference procedures, including how the components are optimized and reused.

A. Experimental Setup 1) Datasets: we evaluate effectiveness and efficiency of FuseRAG on three public multihop Question Answering (QA) datasets, including MusiQue [30], HotpotQA [31] and 2Wiki-MultiHopQA [32]: MuSiQue consists of questions constructed by composing single-hop questions into multi-hop reasoning chains. HotpotQA contains questions that require reasoning over multiple Wikipedia articles and provides sentence to support facts. 2WikiMultiHopQA combines information from Wikipedia and Wikidata to construct questions with explicit reasoning paths. These datasets are widely utilized in graph-based RAG. Following prior work [33]–[37], 1,000 questions are sampled from the validation split of each dataset. Mean-while, we adopt two open-source backbone LLMs: Llama-3-8B and Qwen3.5-9B. Llama-3-8B has been widely adopted in recent RAG studies, facilitating comparison with prior work. Qwen3.5-9B is a newly released model that has attracted con-siderable attention, allowing us to further assess effectiveness. 

2) Metrics: a) Effectiveness Metrics: We report accuracy (Acc) and F1. Acc measures the proportion of questions for which the generated answer contains a reference answer. F1 measures the token-level overlap between the generated and reference answers and is computed as the harmonic mean of precision and recall. We report both metrics averaged over the evaluated questions. 

b) Efficiency Metrics: Efficiency is measured by token consumption and runtime latency. We primarily focus online query processing, following the existing works. Token con-sumption includes input and output tokens. Runtime latency is the total time required to construct the index and the average end-to-end latency per online query. We additionally report time to first token (TTFT), defined as the time costs from query submission until the first answer token is produced. TTFT captures the initial response delay, while end-to-end latency reflects the total time required to complete a query. 

3) Comparison Methods: We compare FuseRAG with two basic baselines and a range of graph-based RAG methods, covering the classic approches and the state-of-the-art (SOTA) 

methods recently:  

> •

Zero-shot. The backbone LLM directly answers each question without retrieving external evidence. This base-line evaluates the model’s ability to answer questions using its internal knowledge.  

> •

VanillaRAG. A conventional RAG baseline retrieves text chunks based on their semantic similarity to the query and provides the retrieved chunks to the LLM for answer generation.  

> •

KGP. Knowledge Graph Prompting constructs a graph over document structures, with edges capturing semantic and structural associations. An LLM-based agent navi-gates the graph to collect supporting passages for answer generation.  

> •

ToG. An KG-based GraphRAG for online query reason-ing. ToG conducts inference on manually designed KG.  

> •

RAPTOR. RAPTOR recursively embeds, clusters, and summarizes text chunks to construct a hierarchical tree. It retrieves information at different levels of abstrac-tion, combining detailed passages with higher-level sum-maries.  

> •

G-Retriever. G-Retriever retrieves a query-relevant sub-graph from a textual graph and integrates graph represen-tations with an LLM through soft prompting for answer generation.  

> •

LGraphRAG. The local search variant of GraphRAG retrieves query-relevant entities and combines their as-sociated graph information with supporting text chunks to construct the context for answer generation.  

> •

FastGraphRAG. FastGraphRAG uses personalized PageRank to explore a knowledge graph and identify relevant evidence for answering the query.  

> •

LLightRAG. The local retrieval mode of LightRAG focuses on query-relevant entities and their associated in-formation, providing context for questions about specific facts.  

> •

GLightRAG. The global retrieval mode of LightRAG focuses on relationships associated with high-level query keywords, retrieving broader relational context.  

> •

HLightRAG. The hybrid retrieval mode of LightRAG combines local and global retrieval results, incorporating both entity-specific information and broader relational context.  

> •

GFM-RAG. GFM-RAG employs a pretrained graph neural network to model query-knowledge relationships and retrieve supporting evidence through reasoning over graph structure.  

> •

KET-RAG. KET-RAG constructs a knowledge graph skeleton from selected text chunks and a lightweight text– keyword bipartite graph over the corpus. Retrieval com-bines evidence from both structures to balance retrieval quality and indexing cost.

Query conditioning and graph fusion serve distinct roles within the latent branch. Query conditioning directs atten-tion pooling toward question-relevant evidence tokens and conditions cross-passage message computation on the current question. Removing it lowers F1 by 6.94% and accuracy by 8.39%, with only a 1.01 × speedup. This suggests that adapting evidence representations to the question is important for effective memory use. Graph fusion allows connected pas-sages to exchange information and produces auxiliary struc-tural representations alongside the original evidence states. Without it, F1 decreases to 26.48 and accuracy to 22.20%, while the speedup is limited to 1.03 ×. Thus, structural fusion provides additional benefits beyond independently encoded, query-conditioned passage representations, at a relatively small observed latency cost. Memory granularity also affects the quality–efficiency trade-off. Replacing token-level memory with passage-level memory gains a 1.33 × speedup, but reduces F1 to 23.91% and accuracy to 20.45%. Token-level memory exposes multiple evidence positions to the decoder, enabling selective access to finer-grained information rather than relying on a passage-level summary. A. Graph-based RAG 

Graph-based RAG organizes external corpora through en-tities, relationships, and associations, thereby capturing de-pendencies that go beyond isolated text blocks [38], [39]. A majority of existing work focuses on leveraging hierarchical or relational graph structures to enhance the indexing and retrieval processes [40]–[42]. For example, RAPTOR [43] recursively embeds, clusters, and summarizes text blocks to construct a hierarchical retrieval tree. GraphRAG [44] and LightRAG [45] construct graphs and community summaries to support local, global, and hybrid retrieval modes for document collections. To balance indexing overhead and improve multi-hop retrieval, recent methods such as HippoRAG [33], KET-RAG [46], and Clue-RAG [37] explore more efficient graph retrieval and indexing strategies. These methods adopt person-alized PageRank, knowledge graph skeletons combined with text-keyword bipartite graphs, and multi-partite graph indexes connecting text chunks, knowledge units, and entities [47]. Another perspective focuses on performing explicit traversal or multi-step reasoning directly on graph structures during the online reasoning phase [48]. Typical methods include KGP [49] using LLM agents for graph navigation, ToG [50] iterative reasoning on knowledge graphs and GFM-RAG [8], which utilizes pre-trained graph neural networks to model query-knowledge relationships. Despite their strong retrieval capabilities, most graph-based RAG methods still serialize graph evidence [51], leading to structural degradation and positional biases. G-Retriever [52] mitigates this issue through graph-based soft prompts but does not explicitly preserve reusable token-level evidence. In contrast, FuseRAG separates reusable evidence content from query-dependent graph interactions and integrates both into decoder attention alongside compact textual anchors. 

B. Latent Evidence Integration 

Encoding external context into continuous latent repre-sentations offers a highly promising technical approach as an alternative to processing full-length text. Early research, such as Prefix-Tuning [53], AutoCompressors [54], and ICAE [55], used autoencoder or language modeling objectives to compress long contexts into compact summary vectors or a fixed number of memory slots, which served as soft prompts to reduce the computational overhead of self-attention [56]. In RAG scenarios, xRAG [57] uses a learned modality bridge to directly project pre-trained offline document embeddings into the representation space of large language models, thereby en-abling the reuse of offline document embeddings and reducing the consumption of text tokens. Relatively few studies have explored latent representations for graph-based RAG. Exist-ing approaches often compress evidence into a few vectors, potentially losing fine-grained semantics, while overlooking graph relationships. In contrast, FuseRAG integrates evidence semantics and graph structure in latent space, supporting multi-hop reasoning without full textual serialization. 

C. Efficient RAG Inference 

Accelerating the prefill phase of large language models in RAG scenarios has become a critical research focus in the systems community, with mainstream approaches centering on the cross-query reuse of KV caches [58]–[60]. Systems such as RAGCache [61] organize intermediate knowledge states in a knowledge tree and manage cache reuse for shared prefixes across GPU and host memory. Since standard KV-cache reuse requires strict exact token-prefix matching, recent research has explored reuse mechanisms that overcome these prefix constraints [62], [63]. For example, CacheBlend [64] combines precomputed chunk KV caches with selective token recomputation to restore cross-chunk attention interactions between the current block and its preceding context, while Cache-Craft [65] further evaluates chunk-cache reusability to jointly schedule selective recomputation, storage, and eviction strategies. Furthermore, recent efforts such as MatKV [66] and SubGCache [67] extend caching efficiency by leveraging matrix-level KV transformations for modular state alignment or exploiting overlapping subgraph structures to facilitate reuse in graph-based retrieval. Existing caching methods primarily reuse or adapt decoder KV states derived from serialized text. However, varying graph structures and serialization orders complicate such reuse in graph-based RAG. In contrast, FuseRAG caches query-independent evidence representations and incorporates graph interactions in latent space, reducing redundant computation and prefill overhead without requiring identical token prefixes. VII. C ONCLUSION 

We presented FuseRAG, an efficient graph-based RAG framework that integrates retrieved evidence and structural interactions into decoder attention. Its dual-path design com-bines compact textual anchors with latent evidence, preserving independently encoded passage states while augmenting them with question-dependent graph representations. By reusing evidence states across queries and memory K/V across decod-ing steps, FuseRAG reduces repeated encoding and long-text prefill without requiring the complete retrieved graph to be verbalized. Experiments across three multi-hop QA datasets and two LLM backbones demonstrate favorable trade-offs between answer quality and inference efficiency. These results support latent evidence integration as a practical direction for efficient graph-based RAG. REFERENCES [1] Y. Chang, X. Wang, J. Wang, Y. Wu, L. Yang, K. Zhu, H. Chen, X. Yi, C.
