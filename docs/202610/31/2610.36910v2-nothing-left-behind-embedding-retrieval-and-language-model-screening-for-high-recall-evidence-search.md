## 摘要
基于检索文档为其答案提供依据的 AI 系统通常只需要少量相关段落，但系统综述和法律证据开示等应用需要几乎所有相关段落，因为其结论依赖于完整的证据体。这些应用传统上使用人工整理分类体系进行检索，而分类体系只能找到其词表所描述的内容。我们在 ImpactAI（一个基于 7,354 项随机试验构建的证据合成应用）中，将基于分类体系的检索与一种替代性神经检索进行比较；后者基于句子嵌入，并随后进行语言模型筛选。以 29 篇系统综述所纳入的研究为基准衡量，检出的研究比例从 45% 提高到 85%，并且筛选模型与人工标注者之间的一致性，与标注者彼此之间的一致性相当。这一提升以速度为代价：中位数情况下，针对一个查询检索并筛选证据需要 77 秒，而基于分类体系的检索仅需 6 秒。结果表明，可以使用嵌入来发现证据，用分类标签来组织证据，并根据每个查询可用的时间设定需要筛选的候选数量。

## Abstract
AI systems that ground their answers from retrieved documents usually need only a few relevant passages, but applications such as systematic reviews and legal discovery need nearly all of them, because their conclusions rest on the full body of evidence. These applications have traditionally searched with curated taxonomies, which can only find what their vocabulary describes. We compare taxonomy-based retrieval with an alternative neural retrieval based on sentence embeddings and followed by language model screening in ImpactAI, an evidence synthesis application built on 7,354 randomized trials. Measured against the studies included in 29 systematic reviews, the share of studies found rises from 45% to 85%, and the screening model agrees with human annotators as well as annotators agree with each other. This gain comes at a cost in speed: at the median, retrieving and screening evidence for a query takes 77 seconds, against 6 seconds for taxonomy retrieval. The results suggest finding evidence with embeddings, organizing it with taxonomy labels, and setting how many candidates to screen according to the time available for each query.
