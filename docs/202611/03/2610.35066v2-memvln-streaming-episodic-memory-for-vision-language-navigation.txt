ABSTRACT 

Vision-language navigation (VLN) requires an embodied agent to follow language instructions through 3D environments. Maintaining persistent episodic memory remains a challenge for VLM-based streaming agents in long-horizon navigation. We present M EM VLN, a streaming episodic memory framework that maintains a bounded bank of compressed perceptual features annotated with timesteps and action-derived relative poses, and produces compact memory tokens via cross-attention. We pair the proposed memory with a frozen pretrained geometry encoder. To learn the memory module, we design a masking-based trajectory summarization task with multi-granularity sub-trajectory decomposition, encouraging route recon-struction exclusively from episodic memory. Under navigation training restricted to the official R2R/RxR trajectories, M EM VLN achieves state-of-the-art performance on most reported metrics on standard VLN-CE benchmarks. Long-horizon analy-ses further characterize when episodic memory is useful. For physical evaluation, we collect 30 instruction-conditioned episodes in one physical scene, use 24 as demonstrations for post-training, and hold out six for testing. On the held-out episodes, M EM VLN improves success over the VLM baseline from 17% to 40%, and post-training further raises it to 47%. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Vision-language navigation (VLN) is a fundamental task in embodied AI, requiring an embodied agent to follow language instructions through 3D environments (Anderson et al., 2018b; Krantz et al., 1arXiv:2610.35066v2 [cs.CV] 27 Oct 2026 2020; Majumdar et al., 2023). Recent vision-language models (VLMs) have substantially improved navigation performance, with monocular streaming video as a prevailing formulation that is natural and practical for real-world deployment (Zhang et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026b;a). Despite rapid progress, the lack of persistent memory remains a critical constraint for real-world long-horizon navigation, where decisions depend on observations from earlier in the episode. In this paper, we address the key problem: how to design a simple yet effective episodic memory that integrates seamlessly with streaming VLM to elevate VLN capability ?Navigation memory has received prior attention, yet existing approaches remain insufficient along several key dimensions. Early methods encode navigation history in recurrent hidden states (Elman, 1990; Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997; Hong et al., 2021), compressing perceptual observations into a fixed-size representation. Explicit memory representations retain observation-level features but are often coupled to panoramic observations or topological maps, requiring adaptation to monocular streaming video in continuous environments (Chen et al., 2021b; Mezghan et al., 2022; An et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023b; Yu et al., 2025). More recent works revisit navigation memory within a streaming formulation (Zeng et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2025a), yet the capacity limitation persists. These shortcomings motivate our approach: an episodic memory design that is expressive and natively compatible with streaming video, accompanied by a systematic study of navigation memory spanning long-horizon navigation scenarios and real-world validation .We propose M EM VLN, a streaming episodic memory framework that endows VLMs with persistent navigation memory. M EM VLN maintains dense short-term observations for immediate action while building a compact, queryable summary of the accumulated episode history. Concretely, a memory bank progressively stores perceptual features of streaming observations alongside their timestep and action-derived relative pose. For action prediction, a memory perceiver extracts latent features from the bank and encodes them with the corresponding spatio-temporal context. It then derives a fixed-size set of memory tokens via cross-attention with learnable queries grounded in the current timestep and relative pose. Memory tokens enter the VLM’s multi-modal sequence for auto-regressive action prediction. When the bank reaches its capacity limit, a frame-merging strategy consolidates consecutive frames into a bounded episodic summary. As a complementary source of spatial features, we use a frozen pretrained StreamVGGT encoder (Zhuo et al., 2026) and train a lightweight adapter to fuse its features into the VLM. Both the memory module and the geometry encoder integrate into existing VLMs for streaming video without modifying the underlying LLM architecture. To supervise memory learning, we design a dedicated auxiliary task alongside navigation. Specifically, we adopt trajectory summarization (Zheng et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024a; Cheng et al., 2025) as the supervision for the memory module, where local visual tokens are masked, leaving memory tokens as the visual evidence for describing the traversed path. To enrich the supervisory signal, we further decompose each full trajectory into hierarchically structured sub-trajectories (Hong et al., 2020), yielding fine-grained supervision across multiple temporal scales. Together, the memory module design, multi-granularity trajectory summarization, and masking strategy constitute a principled framework for learning navigation memory. We conduct extensive experiments in simulation and on a physical robot. On standard VLN-CE benchmarks (Anderson et al., 2018b; Ku et al., 2020; Krantz et al., 2020), M EM VLN reaches at least 55% success on both R2R and RxR and achieves state-of-the-art performance on most metrics among methods whose navigation fine-tuning trajectories are restricted to the official R2R/RxR training splits. Ablations isolate the contributions of memory, geometry, and auxiliary supervision, while long-horizon analyses further examine when memory is useful. For physical evaluation on the G1 robot, we collect 30 episodes in one physical scene, use 24 as demonstrations for post-training, and hold out six for testing. On the held-out episodes, M EM VLN improves success over the VLM baseline from 17% to 40%, and post-training further raises it to 47%. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Vision-Language Navigation. VLN requires an embodied agent to follow natural language in-structions through 3D environments. Early works establish VLN as a foundational task for embodied AI (Chang et al., 2017; Zhu et al., 2017; Xia et al., 2018; Anderson et al., 2018a; Savva et al., 2019; Anderson et al., 2018b; Chaplot et al., 2020; Qi et al., 2020; Ku et al., 2020; Krantz et al., 2020; Thomason et al., 2020; Ramakrishnan et al., 2021; Krantz et al., 2023; Yadav et al., 2023). Given 2scarce annotated navigation data, considerable work targets data-centric approaches, including data augmentation (Tan et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2020; Hong et al., 2020; He et al., 2025; Cui et al., 2025) and data scaling in both simulation environments (Hao et al., 2020; Kamath et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023c) and the real world (Guhur et al., 2021; Linghu et al., 2024; Cheng et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026). As model capabilities advance, VLN evaluation encompasses increasingly complex scenarios (Majumdar et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024b; Yokoyama et al., 2024b; Khanna et al., 2024; Qiao et al., 2025a; Lin et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b). In this paper, we focus on the fundamental VLN task in continuous environments and study how navigation memory can be learned and leveraged without relying on massive data scaling (Anderson et al., 2018b; Ku et al., 2020; Krantz et al., 2020; Hong et al., 2020). 

Vision-Language Models for Navigation. Effective VLN requires models to perceive 3D envi-ronments and comprehend natural language instructions. While early approaches trained navigation models from scratch (Fried et al., 2018; Nguyen et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2019; Hao et al., 2020), the strengths of pretrained VLMs have emerged as a more promising pathway towards general-purpose VLN (Majumdar et al., 2022; Gadre et al., 2023; Dorbala et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024a; Long et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024). This paradigm has inspired diverse approaches, including topological structure methods (Chen et al., 2021a; Krantz et al., 2021; Hong et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2022a;b; Yokoyama et al., 2024a; An et al., 2024), explicit 3D spatial modeling (An et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024b; Huang et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025d; 2026), and trajectory-level modeling (Shah & Levine, 2022; Shah et al., 2023; Sridhar et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025c; Gode et al., 2025). More recently, the input formulation has been simplified to monocular streaming video, under which navigation performance has improved rapidly (Zheng et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024a; 2025; Cheng et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026b; Zeng et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2025a; Qi et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026a; Chu et al., 2026), with significant progress also demonstrated in real-world deployment (Gervet et al., 2023; Yue et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024; Qiao et al., 2025b; Sun et al., 2026; Yue et al., 2026; Yu et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026). Despite this progress, directly retaining long visual histories in streaming VLM policies increases input context and computation, motivating dedicated memory mechanisms. 

Memory for Embodied Agents. Memory mechanisms for embodied agents predate modern VLM-based navigation and span several task formulations. Recurrent policies compress navigation history into hidden states (Elman, 1990; Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997; Hong et al., 2021), whereas explicit approaches retain observation histories or structured spatial representations (Chen et al., 2021b; Mezghan et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023b; Yu et al., 2025). SMT appends each observation to a growing within-episode memory for visual navigation, while E.T. jointly encodes language with the full visual-action history for ALFRED (Fang et al., 2019; Pashevich et al., 2021). ESceme instead maintains a cross-episode, location-indexed scene memory for VLN (Zheng et al., 2025b). Beyond navigation, MemoryVLA retrieves decision-relevant history from a perceptual-cognitive memory for robotic manipulation (Shi et al., 2026). In contrast, MemVLN targets a bounded, episode-local visual bank for streaming VLN, with time/pose-grounded latent readout and memory-specific trajectory-summary supervision.

MEM VLN is trained jointly on two tasks: VLN and trajectory summarization. The navigation task teaches the model to predict the next action, while the summarization task shapes the memory representation towards route-level semantics. Both tasks use a unified cross-entropy loss.

4 EXPERIMENTS 

Implementation Details. We use Qwen3-VL-4B (Bai et al., 2025), including its native SigLIP-2-based vision encoder, together with StreamVGGT-1B (Zhuo et al., 2026). Both encoders are frozen. We adapt the LLM with LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) and train the memory module and geometry adapter from scratch, yielding 1B trainable parameters in total. The recent observation window contains up to 16 frames. The model is trained for 1 epoch (2 days on 8 ×A100 80G GPUs) using AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019) with a base learning rate of 2 × 10 −5, linear warmup, and cosine decay. See detailed hyperparameters in Appendix .

Data and Evaluation. We train on the official R2R (Anderson et al., 2018b) and RxR (Ku et al., 2020) training trajectories, comprising 10,819 and 19,996 demonstrations. Full-trajectory summary targets reuse the instructions from these routes; fine-grained R2R supervision uses FGR2R sub-instruction/waypoint annotations derived from R2R routes (Hong et al., 2020). No additional navigation trajectories or external multimodal fine-tuning data are used. For simulation, we roll out the policy in Habitat-Sim (Savva et al., 2019) under the VLN-CE protocol (Krantz et al., 2020) and report Val-Unseen results. For R2R, we report success rate (SR), oracle success rate (OSR), success weighted by path length (SPL), and navigation error (NE). For RxR, we report SR, SPL, normalized dynamic time warping (nDTW) (Ilharco et al., 2019), and NE. 4.1 MAIN SIMULATION RESULTS 

Comparison Baselines. We compare primarily against recent VLM-based VLN methods, empha-sizing the streaming-video formulation (Wei et al., 2026b; Zeng et al., 2026). For methods with multiple training configurations, Tabs. 1 and 2 report the selected configuration and its additional training-example count. Our state-of-the-art comparison is restricted to methods whose navigation fine-tuning trajectories come only from the official R2R/RxR training splits.

5 CONCLUSION 

MEM VLN combines plug-and-play episodic memory with a frozen pretrained geometry encoder for streaming vision-language navigation. Multi-granularity trajectory summarization trains the bounded memory representation, masking local visual tokens to reconstruct routes exclusively from episodic memory. With navigation training restricted to official R2R/RxR trajectories, M EM VLN achieves state-of-the-art performance on most reported VLN-CE metrics. Simulation analyses characterize memory’s utility across instruction complexities and navigation scenarios. In one physical scene, post-training on 24 demonstrations improves success on six held-out episodes. 

6 LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

First, inference-time retrieval and similarity-based consolidation are instruction-agnostic, so the bounded bank may discard task-relevant observations in long episodes. Second, while trajectory summarization supervises memory learning, broader training strategies remain under-explored (Zheng et al., 2025c). Finally, the real-world study evaluates six held-out episodes in one scene, with substantial collision rates; cross-scene generalization is untested. Future work will investigate task-aware consolidation, broader deployment settings, and safety-aware post-training. 9AI U SE STATEMENT

Streaming Episode Sampler. Training a stateful memory policy requires temporally coherent data loading. Standard random sampling breaks an episode into unrelated steps, preventing the memory bank from being updated in the same chronological order encountered at inference time. We therefore employ a streaming episode sampler that assigns complete episodes to each device and feeds the steps within each episode sequentially. This protocol allows each device to maintain continuous memory streams while supporting distributed training and task mixing. 

Default Memory Configuration. Following the memory module formulation in the main paper, the memory bank stores the visual feature 𝑓 𝑡 of each observation together with its timestep 𝑡 and relative pose 𝑧 𝑡 ; at policy inference, 𝑧 𝑡 is derived from the executed action history. We denote the maximum number of retained frame entries as 𝑁 𝑏 . Each frame is spatially compressed to at most 𝑁 𝑓 

patch tokens using ToMe (Bolya et al., 2023). A new observation is first appended to the bank. If this increases the bank size to 𝑁 𝑏 + 1, frame merging consolidates the two consecutive entries with the highest average patch-level cosine similarity, reducing the bank size by one and restoring the capacity constraint. The memory perceiver then uses 𝑁 𝑚 learnable queries to retrieve a fixed-size set of memory tokens 𝑚 𝑡 . Before each memory readout, the current visual feature is added to the bank; the queries are conditioned only on the current timestep and action-derived relative pose, while the instruction is introduced downstream by the LLM. Since each query produces one output token, 

𝑁 𝑚 is also the number of memory tokens injected into the VLM. The default configuration uses

Fixed Token Interface. For one R2R decision with a 16-frame recent-context window, Base receives 2,819 input tokens and Full M EM VLN receives 3,126. In both cases, 15 history frames are padded to 16 and grouped into eight temporal units; these units and the current image each contribute 300 visual tokens, for (8 + 1) × 300 = 2,700 visual tokens. Full M EM VLN additionally contributes 300 fixed memory tokens and 7 associated prompt/special tokens, a fixed increase of 307 tokens under the evaluated prompt format. Other text tokens depend on the instruction. The bounded raw bank is not concatenated into the VLM context, so this memory-side input increment does not grow with route length.

C.3 REAL -WORLD RESULTS 

Evaluation Protocol. We provide a detailed breakdown of the real-world G1 evaluation summarized in Sec. 4.4. All 30 instruction-conditioned episodes were collected in the same physical scene and split at the episode level: 24 demonstrations are used for post-training and six distinct episodes are held out for evaluation. We evaluate Base, +Mem, MemVLN, and MemVLN-PT, repeating each held-out instruction five times for 30 trials per method. In addition to success rate (SR), we report collision rate (CR), the fraction of trials involving physical contact, contact-induced blockage, or contact-induced termination; CR measures contact safety independently of task completion. 

Deployment Setup. During real-world deployment, policy inference runs on a host workstation equipped with a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. RGB observations captured by the robot-mounted Intel RealSense D455 are transmitted to the host over Wi-Fi; after the host completes model inference, the predicted action is sent back to the G1 robot for execution. 

Overall Performance. As shown in Fig. 4, Base succeeds in 5 of 30 runs, corresponding to 16.7% SR. Adding the memory module increases success to 9 of 30 runs (30.0% SR), and the full MemVLN model further improves success to 12 of 30 runs (40.0% SR). After real-world post-training with 24 demonstrations, MemVLN-PT succeeds in 14 of 30 runs (46.7% SR), giving two additional successful runs over MemVLN. CR remains unchanged at 46.7% for MemVLN and MemVLN-PT. 

Instruction-level Analysis. As shown in Tab. 11, MemVLN-PT improves SR over MemVLN on I3 and I5, leaves it unchanged on I2 and I4, and decreases it on I1 and I6. Post-training lowers CR on I2 and I6 and increases it on I1 and I3. All four methods obtain 0% SR on I4, which requires selecting the second door on the right after passing an intermediate door. 

Remaining Challenges. Collision remains a challenge: MemVLN and MemVLN-PT both have a CR of 46.7%. Post-training improves aggregate SR but leaves aggregate CR unchanged. Future work will explore contact-aware control and richer supervision for fine-grained spatial grounding. 21 C.4 MORE QUALITATIVE RESULTS
